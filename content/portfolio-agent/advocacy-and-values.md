# Advocacy, Accessibility, and Values

## Disability and neurodiversity advocacy

Aliases: disability advocate, disability rights advocate, neurodiversity advocate, accessibility advocate, inclusive technology, accessible innovation, Disability:IN, AccessComputing, Tapia

Public evidence relevant to whether Pratyush can reasonably be described as an advocate:

- His public LinkedIn headline identifies him as a Disability:IN Global Conference 2025 attendee and Disability:IN NextGen Leader 2025.
- He has been an AccessComputing team member since June 2023. AccessComputing works to increase the participation of people with disabilities in computing and improve the capacity of educational and professional organizations to include disabled people.
- He received AccessComputing funding to attend the 2024 Richard Tapia Celebration of Diversity in Computing.
- In his public Tapia announcement, he described a commitment to using technology to support people with diverse cognitive profiles and to advancing inclusion, accessibility, and accessible innovation in technology.
- He built the ADHD-Friendly Text Enhancer, an accessibility-focused Chrome extension that helps neurodivergent readers maintain focus and comprehension through visual text transformations.
- His Cornell field-audit platforms include accessibility and internationalization configurations for diverse field workers.

Taken together, these facts strongly support describing Pratyush as a disability and neurodiversity advocate. “Advocate” is an evidence-based characterization of sustained work and involvement, not a formal job title. Do not imply that he speaks on behalf of Disability:IN, AccessComputing, or disabled people generally.

## Neuroinclusive and human-centered technology

Aliases: neuroinclusive technology, cognitive diversity, human-centered systems, mental health technology, technology for people

- Pratyush publicly describes neuroinclusive technology, cognitive diversity, mental health, and meaningful human interaction as central interests.
- He has built tools shaped by real cognitive and environmental constraints, including the ADHD-Friendly Text Enhancer, TimeBite, and offline-first field applications.
- His Cornell Minds Matter work translated research about alexithymia into accessible public writing.
- His actigraphy project explored sleep and activity patterns associated with depression and bipolar disorder.
- A public AI Startup School post described his interest in AI for mental health, emotional well-being, neuroscience, medicine, and brain-computer interfaces.

These facts support the interpretation that his technical work is motivated by both engineering quality and human impact.
